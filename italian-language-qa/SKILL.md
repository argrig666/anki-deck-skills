# Italian Language QA

Apply this checklist to every Italian linguistic field before a card is accepted.

## Orthography

- Verify accents and apostrophes: `è/e`, `l’acqua`, `un po’`, and similar contrasts.
- Check doubled consonants, elision, capitalization, punctuation, and spacing.
- Keep Italian quotation and punctuation conventions consistent with the deck.

## Grammar

- Confirm article, gender, number, agreement, conjugation, tense, mood, and person.
- Check preposition selection and contractions (`a + il = al`, etc.).
- Check pronoun placement, clitic order, negation, and required agreement.
- For examples, verify that the sentence is grammatical without relying on an English-shaped
  construction.

## Meaning and usage

- Verify that the translation matches the intended sense in context.
- Flag false friends, polysemy, register, idiomaticity, and regional variation.
- Prefer natural contemporary Italian over literal translations.
- Label uncommon, literary, archaic, technical, or regional forms.
- Preserve established Latin or other foreign-origin expressions that are conventional
  in English (for example, *a fortiori*) when they add a genuine English usage or
  register distinction. Do not remove them merely because a plainer English paraphrase
  also exists; instead, flag obscure, literal, or non-English equivalents.

## Pedagogical QA (Didactic Value-Added Examples)

- Ensure the prompt tests one thing and gives enough context to disambiguate it.
- Avoid clues that reveal the answer unnecessarily.
- Include gender/article or verb information when omission would create ambiguity.
- **High Didactic Value (Core Deck Principle)**: Example sentences must never be throwaway filler. In the ideal case, the example must:
  1. Exemplify the **authentic, natural usage** of the target word/expression in contemporary Italian.
  2. **Reinforce grammatical intuition**: actively embed and model governing prepositions (valency), subjunctive triggers, clitic pronoun mechanics, auxiliary choices, or agreement patterns.
  3. **Expose the learner to useful phrasing**: introduce or weave in common, natural connectors and idioms (*in modo da*, *a quanto pare*, *non poter fare a meno di*, *prima di + inf*, *senza che + cong*).
- **`-ire` verbs with `-isc-`**: For third-conjugation verbs that insert the `-isc-` infix in conjugated forms (e.g. *capire*, *finire*, *preferire*, *spedire*, *costruire*, *chiarire*, *unire*), the Italian example sentence must use one of the forms featuring `-isc-` (present indicative or subjunctive 1st/2nd/3rd sing. or 3rd plur.: *capisco*, *finisce*, *preferiscono*) rather than an infinitive, compound tense, or non-infix form. This gives the example crucial didactic value by actively illustrating that the verb belongs to the `-isc-` class.

## Final review

For each note, check: Italian text, translation, part of speech, morphology, register,
example sentence, and tags. If a fact is uncertain, mark it for verification rather than
inventing an answer.
