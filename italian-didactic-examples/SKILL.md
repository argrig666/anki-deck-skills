# Italian Didactic Examples

Every Italian example sentence (`ItalianExample`) in the deck must do more than simply contain the headword. It is a fundamental principle of this deck that every example serves as a **high-value learning tool** that actively builds linguistic competence and intuitive mastery of Italian.

## Core Principles

### 1. Authentic, Natural Usage (First Priority)
- The example sentence must reflect authentic, contemporary, idiomatic Italian as spoken and written by native speakers.
- Avoid contrived sentences, literal English calques, or artificial "classroom Italian" assembled merely to hold the target word.
- The target word should appear in its most typical syntactic environment, natural collocation, and appropriate register.

### 2. Reinforcing Grammatical Intuition
Whenever possible, structure the sentence to reinforce subconscious grammatical reflexes and patterns:
- **Prepositional valency**: Clearly demonstrate the governing prepositions that verbs, nouns, or adjectives take (e.g. *interessarsi a/di*, *rinunciare a*, *dipendere da*, *collegare a*, *assistere a*).
- **Subjunctive triggers**: When using verbs of emotion, belief, doubt, opinion, desire, or impersonal constructs (*credere che*, *sembrare che*, *prima che*, *purché*, *benché*, *sperare che*), pair them naturally with the congiuntivo (*spero che tu guarisca*, *benché sia difficile*).
- **Conjugation class showcases**: For third-conjugation (`-ire`) verbs that insert the `-isc-` infix (e.g. *capire*, *finire*, *preferire*, *spedire*, *costruire*, *chiarire*, *unire*), **always** use a conjugated form featuring `-isc-` (*capisco*, *finisce*, *preferiscono*) rather than an infinitive, compound tense, or non-infix form.
- **Pronominal & clitic mechanics**: Showcase natural placement of clitic pronouns (*ci*, *ne*, direct/indirect, combined clitics) and pronominal/reflexive verbs (*rendersi conto*, *accorgersene*, *farcela*, *andarsene*).
- **Agreement & auxiliary selection**: Illustrate proper past participle agreement (e.g. with preceding direct object clitics: *l'ho vista*, *li abbiamo comprati*) and natural auxiliary selection (*essere* vs *avere*).

### 3. Exposing to Common, Useful Structures & Phrasing
Weave in authentic Italian sentence connectors, idiomatic framing, and collocations that broaden the learner's functional repertoire:
- **Connective phrases & transitionals**: *a quanto pare*, *in modo da*, *non poter fare a meno di*, *tanto vale*, *a tal punto che*, *dal momento che*, *nel frattempo*, *in confronto a*.
- **Subordinate clauses & infinitival frames**: *prima di + infinitive*, *senza che + subjunctive*, *invece di + infinitive*, *a patto che + subjunctive*, *dopo aver + past participle*.
- **Natural lexical collocations**: Pair nouns and verbs as native speakers do (*prendere una decisione*, *fare una domanda*, *tenere d'occhio*, *avere a che fare con*, *fare brutta figura*).

### 4. Maximizing Didactic Value (No "Throwaway" Sentences)
- **Zero triviality**: Strictly avoid empty, minimalist filler sentences (e.g., *"Vedo il tavolo"*, *"Il cane mangia"*).
- **Dual learning impact**: The sentence must test the headword on the reverse card while simultaneously providing rich surrounding context that teaches or consolidates grammar, syntax, and vocabulary.
- **Contextual disambiguation**: The sentence must provide enough context to unmistakably indicate the intended sense of polysemous or nuanced words.

### 5. Stress Marking Consistency
- As required across the deck, mark primary stress on polysyllabic words using diacritics (`è/é`, `ò/ó`, `à`, `ì`, `ù`) according to vowel openness and deck conventions.
